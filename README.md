## Global Development & Macroeconomic Insights: Unlocking 58 Years of Global Progress (1960–2018)

---
## Short Description

This project transforms a massive historical dataset tracking 211 unique nations over a continuous 58-year timeline into an interactive, executive-ready data story. Curated from Maven Analytics (originally sourced from the World Bank and International Labour Organization), this Power BI dashboard bypasses static regional snapshots to uncover deep, multi-decade macro-level trajectories. By employing advanced DAX modeling and robust statistical auditing, the dashboard reveals the changing rules of human longevity, digital transformation, and economic scaling in the modern world.

---
## Tech Stack & Architecture

The entire data lifecycle—from raw transactional logs to front-end interactive visuals—was engineered natively within the Microsoft Power BI ecosystem:

**ETL & Data Engineering:** Advanced Power Query (M) utilized for text normalization, geographic alignment, and isolation of historical reporting anomalies.

**Data Modeling:** Optimized, single-direction Star Schema designed for lightning-fast file execution and zero cross-filtering errors.

**Analytical Calculations:** Custom Data Analysis Expressions (DAX) engineered to handle dynamic time-series aggregation and rolling global metrics.

**Front-End Interface:** High-contrast corporate design featuring dark navy accent banners, dynamic tooltips, conditional heatmap matrices, and integrated Choropleth mapping.

---
## Project Goals

Map Long-Term Wealth Trajectories: Isolate non-linear development curves across diverse economic blocks over a half-century of history.

Audit Global Health Lifelines: Evaluate macro structural shifts in life expectancy and infant mortality to isolate global standards of care.

Uncover Structural Decoupling: Track the alignment between cross-border technological adoption and the physical constraints of national power grids.

Eliminate Outlier Skewing: Replace traditional arithmetic averages with dynamic median calculations to ensure small nations are not statistically overshadowed by regional economic giants.

---
## Macroeconomic Questions Answered

This dashboard transitions from basic descriptive tracking to true diagnostic analytics by explicitly answering the following critical macroeconomic questions:

**1. Does increased national wealth indefinitely translate into human longevity?**

The Preston Curve Model: No. The dashboard's bivariate scatter profiling demonstrates clear diminishing returns. Minor wealth increases in low-income nations prompt explosive gains in life expectancy as basic public utilities are secured. However, once a nation crosses into high-income tiers, the returns curve flattens completely, shifting the structural burden toward managing aging demographics.

**2. Has the global digital explosion overloaded the world's power grids?**

Answered by The Energy Decoupling View: Surprisingly, no. By tracking internet penetration alongside per capita electricity consumption from 1999 to 2018, the dashboard exposes a historic structural decoupling. While internet usage scaled exponentially, power consumption plateaued due to modern micro-infrastructure and data center efficiencies.

**3. Does a booming macroeconomic output automatically clear domestic joblessness?**

Answered by Labor Market Tracking: No. The labor market scatter matrices reveal that advanced high-GDP nations maintain highly persistent, rigid structural unemployment bands. This proves that economic scale and active employment optimization require entirely separate policy and training levers.

**4. How long does it take for a society to stabilize after a healthcare revolution?**
   
Answered by The Demographic Transition Layer: The data captures a distinct multi-decade adjustment lag. Modern health interventions trigger an immediate, sharp drop in infant mortality rates. However, regional birth rates adjust much more slowly due to entrenched cultural frameworks, creating the exact population surges that defined late 20th-century developing spaces.

Dataset provided by Maven Analytics. Original administrative data compiled by the World Bank Group and the International Labour Organization (ILO).
